# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three roughly equal primary segments arrive via local Salt Lake City search, mostly on mobile, often with same-day intent:

1. **Solo client** — stressed or sore, comparing spas on Google ("massage near me"), wants quick legible booking and trustworthy reviews.
2. **Couple planning a date-night experience** — couples massage, head spa, sauna add-on. Browsing for ambiance, occasion fit, gift potential.
3. **Gift-giver** — buying a gift card or experience for a partner, parent, or friend. Cares about presentation, ease of purchase, redemption clarity.

Job-to-be-done across all three: compare quickly → trust the place → book without friction. Most decisions happen on a phone, often within 24 hours of wanting an appointment.

## Product Purpose

Elite Spa Utah is a Salt Lake City massage therapy and spa at 1136 S State Street. The website's job is to convert local search traffic into booked appointments by:

- Communicating "elevated but approachable" quality
- Surfacing the full service menu (Couples, Deep Tissue, Head Spa, Foot Reflexology, Hot Stone, Cupping, Ashiatsu, Shiatsu, Sports, Swedish, 4-Hands, Medical Infrared Sauna, Chair, Individual)
- Making pricing legible enough that visitors don't bounce
- Handing off cleanly to the talech online scheduler from `/book`

Success is **booked appointments**, not brand impressions. Trust signals + mobile UX matter more than long brand storytelling.

## Brand Personality

**Three words: bright, welcoming, modern.**

- **Voice:** warm and direct, never aloof. Confident without being luxury-distant. Inviting like a boutique neighbor, not gatekeeping like a private club.
- **Tone:** conversational professional. Plain-language descriptions of services. Avoid clinical jargon, avoid breathy "wellness journey" prose.
- **Emotional goal:** a first-time visitor lands and feels "this place is for me, I can just walk in."

## Anti-references

Strongest no: **generic stock-photo medical-spa template** — white robes, cucumber-eyes-on-towel, 2015 corporate stock library, indistinguishable from any chiropractor or dental office. This is the default 90% of spa sites land on.

Also avoid:

- **Saturated wellness-Pinterest aesthetic** — millennial pink, watercolor blobs, swirly script fonts, pastel everything
- **Vegas-spa overdrive** — gold-on-black, Forbes-Travel-Guide signaling, nouveau-riche styling
- **Hotel-chain blandness** — beige Marriott neutrality with no personality

## Design Principles

1. **Same-day intent first.** Most visitors are within 24 hours of wanting an appointment. Booking and price legibility outrank brand storytelling.
2. **Approachable luxury, not aloof luxury.** Communicate quality without distance. Clear prices, clear hours, clear address — these ARE the trust signals.
3. **Three audiences, equal weight.** Solo, couple, gift-giver. No design decision should optimize for one at the cost of another. Service menu and CTAs need to stay legible to all three.
4. **Show, don't perform.** Real photography of the real space and real services beats stock and beats stylized illustration. The space is the differentiator.
5. **Mobile is the primary canvas.** Local same-day search happens on phones. Desktop is the secondary surface, not the design starting point.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG 2.1 AA** baseline: 4.5:1 text contrast, 3:1 UI/large-text contrast, full keyboard navigation, semantic HTML, proper labels.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion` respected** on all transitions and parallax. Migraine-prone and anxiety-prone clients are an actual segment for a spa audience.
- **Mobile-first responsive design** with 44×44px minimum touch targets.
- **Image alt text** reflects actual content (not decorative) for service, staff, and space photos.
- **Forms** (contact, gift card, booking) have explicit labels, clear error messages, and visible focus indicators.
- **Color is never the sole carrier of meaning** — the 8% of male and 0.5% of female visitors with color-vision deficiencies must still be able to navigate.
